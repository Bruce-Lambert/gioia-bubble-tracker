## Key question 1: What wrong-drug / LASA studies appeared 2006–2026 in the named pharmacy-practice journals (and their conference-abstract supplements)?

### Takeaway
The pharmacy-practice journals hold a substantial body of wrong-drug material that a PubMed keyword search misses because the papers are framed as dispensing accuracy, quality-related events (QREs), technician verification, automation, or liability studies rather than as "LASA" studies. The richest unmined veins are (a) regulator/insurer datasets (New Hampshire Board QRE reports, Ontario AIMS, Nova Scotia CPhIR, HPSO/CNA claims), where wrong drug is 19–44% of events; (b) hospital-pharmacy automation evaluations in AJHP/Hosp Pharm/EJHP that report wrong-drug or product-selection error counts with denominators; and (c) IJPP/EJHP conference abstracts (RPS, EAHP) that are indexed only via Consensus/Semantic Scholar.

### Inferences
- Across regulator/insurer datasets the wrong-drug share is remarkably consistent: 19.5% (Ontario AIMS, mixed incidents + near misses), 36.8–43.8% (US liability claims), 40% (New Hampshire Board QREs), 44.4% (Serbian pharmacists' self-reported incidents). Liability and board datasets over-represent wrong drug relative to near-miss datasets, consistent with wrong-drug errors being both more likely to reach patients and more likely to be litigated.
- The pharmacy-automation literature in AJHP/Hosp Pharm/IJPP consistently reports that technology lowers product-selection error rates only when paired with labeling and training changes (Oldland 2015: 0.157% → 0.050% only in the fourth phase; James 2013: 0.64% → 0.28%; Denny 2026: 160 → 5 wrong-drug events), while refill/storage design (Helmons 2012: 70% of incorrect ADC pocket contents in multi-drug drawers; Ruutiainen 2021: 16% of LASA products co-stored) is a repeated wrong-drug mechanism.
- Technician verification studies (TCT/TPV/TAFPV) are a hidden wrong-drug literature: their accuracy endpoints are overwhelmingly product-selection (wrong drug/strength/form) outcomes, and they are not retrieved by LASA/wrong-drug keyword searches.

### Gaps
- No JMCP or JPHSR paper on wrong-drug/LASA errors was retrieved by journal-restricted PubMed search; 340B/specialty-pharmacy wrong-drug data were not found in any named venue.
- IJPP/EJHP conference abstracts are only indexed via Consensus/Semantic Scholar; Mwandiambira 2023 and Lai 2022 lack the wrong-drug subtype counts in the captured text, and Nagamotoo 2022 captured no figures.
- ASHP Midyear abstract PDFs could not be parsed by the query tool (677 and 645 pages); the two snippet leads above are unverified.
- Pharmacy Times and Drug Topics searches returned only commentary; no trade-press reports of original wrong-drug data were located.
- OpenAlex could not be queried (host blocked), so citation-based expansion from the found items was not possible.

### Takeaway
Strong pharmacy-venue evidence exists for technician product-selection accuracy, automation, QRE/claims analyses and look-alike packaging case series; thin or absent evidence exists in pharmacy-practice venues for 340B/specialty, cephalosporin name confusion, telephone-order sound-alike studies, the Emily Jerry case, and controlled pharmacy-student LASA education trials.

### Inferences
- Technician-verification and automation studies supply the best denominators for wrong-drug (product-selection) rates in US hospital pharmacies: 0.05–0.16% of doses at final check (Oldland), 0.21–0.91% of ADC pockets (Helmons), 0–0.5% of fills (Lin), and 0.01–0.085% undetected in community TPV (Hohmeier).
- The claim that automation per se prevents wrong-drug errors is not supported without co-interventions: the only statistically significant reductions occurred after relabeling/training (Oldland) or with full robotics (Lin XR2, Denny barcode+image capture).
- Insulin and biosimilar mix-up evidence in pharmacy-practice venues is almost entirely descriptive or educational; quantitative insulin mix-up data come from ED surveillance (Geller 2021), not pharmacy journals.

### Gaps
- No 340B or specialty-pharmacy wrong-drug study was found in any named venue.
- No pharmacy-venue cephalosporin-confusion or telephone-order sound-alike study was found (2006–2026).
- No controlled trial of LASA education in pharmacy students was found; existing items are descriptive (Chuang 2021) or small pilots (Cheesman 2022, n=26).
- The original Pharmacists Mutual claims study documents were not located; all figures are second-hand and undated.
- The Emily Jerry case appears only in trade/advocacy press, not in the peer-reviewed pharmacy literature retrieved here.
