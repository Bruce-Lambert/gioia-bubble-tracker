## Part A, Q1. Wrong-drug error rates (share of medication errors; per order/dose) in non-Western settings: hospital, community pharmacy, primary care

### Takeaway
Across East Asia, South/Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Africa and Latin America, "wrong drug" is consistently one of the top three error types, but its share varies enormously with the detection method and denominator: roughly 15–20% of incident-reported hospital medication errors (Hong Kong 16.7%; Norway 15% for comparison), 20–50% of anaesthesia drug errors (Thailand 48.8%, Japan 48%, South Africa syringe swaps ~40%), 20–40% of filling/"counselling" errors in pharmacies (Malaysia 39.6% of filling near-misses; Jordan 41.9% of counselling errors), and 10–25% of prescribing errors in Middle-East ambulatory settings (Saudi 10% incorrect drug; Hajj 23%). Per-order dispensing-error rates in Asian hospital pharmacies are very low (0.0063%–0.33 per 10,000 orders), while direct-observation studies in LMIC wards report wrong-drug in up to 21% of orders (Ethiopia).

## Part A, Q2. National LASA lists, Tall Man lettering and name-confusion research outside North America/Western Europe

### Takeaway

## Part B, Q6. AI/NLP/ML: incident-report classification, deep-learning name similarity, computer-vision pill/packaging identification, speech recognition, LLMs (2018–2026)

## Part B, Q7. Patient-facing: patients' detection of wrong drugs, generic appearance changes, app photo verification, QR/smart labels

### Inferences
- Because patients identify medicines by name, LASA *name* confusion is as much a patient-safety problem at home as in the pharmacy; appearance-change notification (Barenie 2020) and app-based verification are complementary but unproven in LMIC settings.

### Gaps
- No study quantifying the proportion of wrong-drug dispensing errors first detected by patients (as opposed to staff) across settings; no RCT of pharmacy-app photo verification on wrong-drug outcomes; no 2021–2023 pill-appearance-change outcome study found.

## Part B, Q8. Veterinary and dental wrong-drug errors (brief)

### Takeaway
Veterinary reporting systems show medication events are the most common patient-safety events, with dose errors dominating and wrong-drug/look-alike (e.g., otic vs ophthalmic products) appearing mainly in poison-control and anaesthesia data; dental literature frames wrong-drug risk within prescribing competence and LASA brand names rather than quantifying rates.

### Cited Findings
- Larson M et al., 2025, J Am Vet Med Assoc, US/Europe: 64,404 voluntarily reported PSEs from 2,284 veterinary facilities (2021–2022); medication-related and patient-handling incidents most common; 6.1% major harm/death. PMID 40306321 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2460/javma.24.08.0523)
- Järvinen A 2026, Acta Vet Scand, Finland: 148 medication errors in 87 malpractice claims (2014–2023); omission most common (16.9%); 21.8% fatal. PMID 42855685 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13028-026-00885-2)
- Barazowski JA 2025, J Am Pharm Assoc: veterinary teaching hospital, 685 ME reports (66% of incidents), 51% wrong dose; gabapentin, methadone, butorphanol most cited. PMID 40015553 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.japh.2025.102362)
- Hepworth-Warren KL 2023, JAVMA: 226 MEs/6,155 large-animal visits; 58.4% prescribing-stage. PMID 38134457 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2460/javma.23.10.0556)
- Pinho RH 2024, Am J Vet Res: perianaesthetic ME rate 1.8% (48/2,728 procedures) in 6 Calgary clinics; 63% wrong dose. PMID 38925141 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2460/ajvr.24.04.0119). Pinho 2024, Vet Anaesth Analg: literature review of veterinary anaesthesia MEs vs human anaesthesia (syringe swaps, look-alike). PMID 38570267 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vaa.2024.01.003)
- Tater K 2023, J Am Anim Hosp Assoc: 79 ASPCA poison-control cases of otic products put in dogs'/cats' eyes; **mistaken identification (otic confused with ophthalmic) in 68/79 (86%)**. PMID 36584316 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5326/JAAHA-MS-7296)

## Part B, Q9. Wrong-drug data from the COVID-19 vaccine and therapeutics era (2021–2023)

### Takeaway
Mass COVID-19 vaccination generated a new wrong-product literature: "incorrect vaccine product" was 20.1% of 209 observed vaccine administration errors in Jordan (point prevalence 2.4%), "type of vaccine used" was the most frequent immunisation error (37.3%) in Brazil's Minas Gerais (7.8/100,000 doses), and "incorrect dosage or product" was 25.9% of 734 CDC NIP-INFO error inquiries; MedDRA coding quality of COVID vaccine error reports was poor. No wrong-drug data specific to COVID therapeutics (e.g., tocilizumab/Paxlovid confusions) were found in PubMed.

### Cited Findings
- Abdel-Qader DH et al., 2025, PLoS One 20(1):e0312050, Jordan: covert observation of 8,743 doses (Nov 2021): 209 VAEs (2.4%); timing 33.0%, dosing 28.7%, **incorrect vaccine product 20.1%**, site/route 8.1%; peak hours aOR 2.18. PMID 39752371 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0312050)
- Ferreira WV et al., 2025, Rev Bras Enferm, Brazil (Minas Gerais, e-SUS Notifica 2021): 2,724 immunisation-error reports; 7.8/100,000 doses; **wrong vaccine type 37.3%**. PMID 40802435 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1590/0034-7167-2023-0525)
- Hall E et al., 2023, Vaccines (CDC): 734 COVID-19 vaccine administration-error inquiries Jan 2021–Apr 2022: storage 26.0%, **incorrect dosage or product 25.9%**, unauthorised age group 14.7%, schedule 14.3%. PMID 36851132 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/vaccines11020254)
- Kralova K et al., 2023, Drug Saf (Sanofi): one-third of MedDRA-coded COVID vaccine medication-error reports inadequately coded; one-third unassessable. PMID 37087705 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40264-023-01294-4)
- Tocco Tussardi I et al., 2022, Int J Environ Res Public Health, Italy (Verona mass vaccination centre): adverse events/near misses over 11 months analysed for recurrent process failures. PMID 35329322 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19063635)
- Klasson H 2026, Public Health Pract, Sweden: inadvertent post-expiry COVID vaccination of 282 seniors. PMID 42182578.
- "Are you vaccinated correctly?" (J Fam Med Prim Care 2022, India): reports of two shots same day with same/different vaccines in Bihar/UP. PMID 35309615 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_1397_21)
- Post-COVID VAERS error-signal analyses using the same methods: McCall KL 2026, Vaccine (vaccination errors in pregnancy; **RSV–wrong product PRR 6.8, ROR 10.3**). PMID 41962228 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vaccine.2026.128570); Ezeja L 2026, Vaccine (RSV vaccine administration errors 2023–2025). PMID 42372626.
- Barceló-Vidal J 2022, Front Pharmacol, Spain: drug-related problems in first-wave COVID inpatients (not wrong-drug specific). PMID 36506516.
- Hause AM MMWR series (2022–2023) and Moro PL 2022 report VAERS safety but not error typology in abstracts (PMIDs 35980875, 36634021, 36327162, 35926203).

### Gaps
- No VAERS-based quantification of COVID-19 "wrong vaccine/wrong product" error counts was retrieved (CDC MMWR abstracts do not report them); ISMP COVID vaccine error newsletters are not PubMed-indexed.
- No study of wrong-drug confusions involving COVID therapeutics (e.g., LASA pairs with remdesivir, tocilizumab, nirmatrelvir) was found.
